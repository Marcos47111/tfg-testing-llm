The integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) as conversational learning assistants introduces notable challenges for software engineering. Unlike conventional software systems governed by deterministic logic, generative language models operate via probabilistic token sampling. This non-deterministic behavior complicates standard boolean assertion testing and represents a direct manifestation of the test oracle problem. In educational environments, such stochastic dynamics often result in plausible hallucinations, security vulnerabilities against adversarial prompt injection, and passive solutionism that hinders student critical thinking.

This Bachelor's Thesis presents and evaluates a testing methodology designed to assess the quality, reliability, and pedagogical utility of LLM-based conversational systems. Grounded in the software product quality attributes of ISO/IEC 25010:2023 and pedagogical concepts such as the Zone of Proximal Development and formative feedback models, the proposed framework establishes seven analytical dimensions. To reduce default midpoint scoring, a discrete four-level rubric ($0$ to $3$) with observable behavioral descriptors is introduced, supported by dimensional metrics, an Educational Quality Index ($IQE$), and a Safety-First decision criterion.

The methodology was evaluated experimentally through a test suite of 42 cases across three chatbot configurations using the Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct model: a general-purpose baseline configuration without educational system instructions ($\text{IQE} = \text{84.1}$), a direct expository tutor ($\text{IQE} = \text{87.9}$), and a Socratic scaffolding tutor ($\text{IQE} = \text{93.0}$). Human expert evaluation established the reference gold standard, revealing that all three configurations exhibited at least one critical failure ($\text{CFR} > 0$), although the Socratic profile achieved the highest global quality score and the strongest transversal pedagogical-feedback performance ($D_4 = \text{2.81}$). As an exploratory extension toward automated auditing, Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct was evaluated as an automated LLM-as-a-Judge across the 126 responses, achieving $63.49\%$ exact agreement, $90.93\%$ proximity within $|\Delta| \le 1$ ($\kappa = 0.190$, $\kappa_{\text{lin}} = 0.280$, $\kappa_{\text{quad}} = 0.364$), and a $50.0\%$ sensitivity in critical failure detection, confirming its viability for preliminary triage while emphasizing the indispensable role of human expert oversight in high-stakes educational deployments.
